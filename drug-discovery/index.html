Fusiomica drug discovery

Our lead binder targets a marker shared by several cancers

We’re developing our own binder for cancer imaging and treatment. We use AI to shorten design rounds. Then we plan a small PET study to see whether the binder reaches tumours in people.

What is theranostics?

Theranostics pairs a scan with a treatment that targets the same marker. A PET tracer shows which tumours take it up and can help doctors select patients for treatment. A therapeutic radioligand binds the same marker and delivers radiation nearby.

  • PET scan
  • Same tumour marker
  • Targeted radiation

We plan to use our lead binder first as a PET tracer. If it reaches tumours in people, we can assess a version that carries a therapeutic isotope.

Pluvicto in prostate cancer

Doctors use PSMA PET to select patients with metastatic prostate cancer for Pluvicto. The treatment binds PSMA and delivers radiation from lutetium-177.

In the VISION trial, median overall survival was 15.3 months with Pluvicto plus standard care and 11.3 months with standard care alone. The FDA expanded its use in 2025. Novartis reported about US$2 billion in sales that year.

What we plan to measure in Phase 0

Once the safety work and approvals are in place, we plan to give a microdose of labelled binder in a supervised PET study. The scan will show how much reaches tumours, where else it goes and how long it stays. A microdose limits exposure and can reduce the preclinical work needed for the first study. The results will help us decide whether to develop a version that delivers therapeutic radiation. Later trials assess treatment benefit.

FDA guidance on microdose radiopharmaceuticals

Why we want human data earlier

Faster design gives us more candidates to work with. A small exploratory study can help us decide which targets are worth taking into larger trials.

Eroom’s law

Historically, the number of drug approvals per billion dollars of R&D spending fell by about half every nine years, after adjusting for inflation. We want to shorten the time between designing a binder and learning where it goes in people.

Scannell et al. on Eroom’s law · What will it take for AI to change drug discovery?

Too many programmes chase the same targets

Many programmes pursue the same targets. A small Phase 0 PET study can test whether a binder reaches tumours in people and where else it accumulates. Those data reduce uncertainty about delivery before we commit to a larger trial. That makes it easier to assess targets that established drug developers may consider too risky.

Bruce Booth on pipeline crowding · LifeSciVC
Delivery and imaging for ADCs and radioligands

A radionuclide decays at a known rate. By measuring uptake and retention, we can estimate the radiation dose to tumours and healthy organs. Treatment response is a separate clinical question.
